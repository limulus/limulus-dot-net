Jon Hoyt [writes about adapting][link] to the changes that AI seems likely to bring to knowledge worker employment, particularly software engineers. It’s worth a read, and so is Justin Searls’ [remarks][via].

One thing that I’m thinking about this morning is “building a small paid problem on the side”:

> Even a small side effort that solves a real problem for real paying customers changes how you see your career. You learn how to talk to buyers, price your work, and deliver value directly instead of relying completely on one employer’s budget spreadsheet. You may never need it to replace your day job, and having that muscle built ahead of time still changes your options.

This is something I’ve toyed with over the years. Even to the point of creating an LLC. But getting to the point of a minimum viable product was insurmountable between my high-quality standards and holding a full-time job.

My experiment with software factories this past week makes me think that since implementation can be delegated to a $100–$200/mo AI subscription, it’s likely worth making another go at an MVP.
